## How to use this guide

Use the sequence as a set of practice prompts, then compare each topic with local responsibilities, authority, and referral routes. The order is an authored learning sequence from the original analysis—not a measured importance, proficiency, or posting-frequency ranking. These prompts do not establish that every topic belongs to every HR Generalist role.

**General next steps:**

- **IC HR practitioner:** Compare local role boundaries and document responsibilities or referrals.
- **Job seeker:** Compare a specific posting with your experience; this desk-research guide is not a universal qualification list.
- **Hiring manager:** Confirm local work, decision authority, and exclusions through a separate job analysis before using this role summary.

## Role label and scope

This guide concerns US individual-contributor HR Generalist work. “People Operations” is retained as part of the project label only; the guide does not assert that titles are interchangeable or establish market-wide title equivalence. Local role ownership varies. No general experience threshold is inferred.

Directors and executive HR leadership, people-manager-only roles, intern/campus roles, and non-US roles are outside the original scope. Recruiting-only work is not represented as the common core; this does not exclude every hiring-related task from every local HR role.

## Authored skill-practice sequence

All five topics below were retained from the original analysis in the same order. Their evidentiary basis is one occupational profile, not employer-posting counts. The source wording is not reproduced here. Evidence strength remains moderate and narrow; no HR-Generalist-specific task-rating records or practitioner validation were admitted.

### 1. Interpret policy and compliance boundaries

Placed first because the occupational profile includes policy and rule-related work; the original author put this boundary before the other HR practice topics. This is a learning-order rationale, not a measured importance rank.

- **IC HR practitioner:** Use an organization-approved source for a policy question, document the applicable rule and your role boundary, and identify the escalation route; do not use this guide as legal advice.
- **Job seeker:** Prepare an anonymized example that names the authoritative rule you consulted, how you explained it, and when you sought review.
- **Hiring manager:** Use a hypothetical policy scenario to check whether a candidate can find approved guidance, separate facts from interpretation, and describe a safe handoff; have qualified reviewers define any assessment.

**Source basis:** O*NET Human Resources Specialists profile, 13-1071.00 (tasks and knowledge sections); see the source register in [the secondary evidence view](evidence-transparency.md).

### 2. Listen carefully and use judgment in employee-relations work

The occupational profile includes employee-relations work. The original author placed this after policy scope because sensitive employee situations call for careful intake and an appropriate next step; no relative-importance score is claimed.

- **IC HR practitioner:** Rehearse a local intake process with a fictional scenario: clarify what happened, record only necessary facts, check confidentiality boundaries, and identify a referral or escalation step.
- **Job seeker:** Use a hypothetical or anonymized example to explain how you listened, checked assumptions, protected confidentiality, and knew when to escalate; do not disclose case details.
- **Hiring manager:** If this work belongs in the role, define observable listening and escalation behaviors and use a consistent, job-related scenario reviewed by appropriate partners.

**Source basis:** O*NET Human Resources Specialists profile, 13-1071.00 (tasks, skills, and transferable-skill sections); see the source register in [the secondary evidence view](evidence-transparency.md).

### 3. Maintain accurate people records and HR documents

The occupational profile includes employee-record and HR-document work. The original author placed this cross-cutting topic after employee-facing practice because it supports controlled transitions and reliable records; no software or proficiency level is inferred.

- **IC HR practitioner:** Walk through an approved document update: verify the source of truth, access permissions, version and approval controls, then check the resulting record without exposing employee data.
- **Job seeker:** Describe a non-confidential example of maintaining records, following version or privacy controls, and catching an error; name only tools the specific employer actually requires.
- **Hiring manager:** Identify the records, documents, system access, retention rules, and decision authority local work requires before setting a selection exercise.

**Source basis:** O*NET Human Resources Specialists profile, 13-1071.00 (tasks and knowledge sections); see the source register in [the secondary evidence view](evidence-transparency.md).

### 4. Explain HR processes clearly to managers

The occupational profile includes manager-facing information or training. The original author placed this after policy, employee-relations, and records practice as a communication application; posting counts were not used to rank it.

- **IC HR practitioner:** Turn an approved process into a short manager briefing: state the next step, documentation needed, boundary of your authority, and escalation route; ask the manager to explain it back.
- **Job seeker:** Prepare a non-confidential example of making a process understandable, tailoring it to the audience, and clarifying what decision remained with the manager.
- **Hiring manager:** If manager enablement is assigned locally, ask for a short explanation of a job-relevant process and evaluate accuracy, clarity, and escalation boundaries consistently.

**Source basis:** O*NET Human Resources Specialists profile, 13-1071.00 (tasks, transferable skills, and abilities sections); see the source register in [the secondary evidence view](evidence-transparency.md).

### 5. Check people data and explain what it can support

The occupational profile includes employment-data and reporting work. The original author placed this after policy, employee-facing, records, and manager-guidance practice as a separate analytic application; no tool, analytic depth, or prevalence is claimed.

- **IC HR practitioner:** Use a non-sensitive reporting example to verify the measure definition, denominator, time window, access rules, and limitations before sharing a finding.
- **Job seeker:** Explain a non-sensitive example of checking a data definition, comparing options, and communicating a finding with its limits; do not claim a tool is universally required.
- **Hiring manager:** Name the actual local reports and access boundaries, then use a consistent work sample only if local job analysis supports it.

**Source basis:** O*NET Human Resources Specialists profile, 13-1071.00 (tasks, skills, and transferable-skill sections); see the source register in [the secondary evidence view](evidence-transparency.md).
