Every skill a job asks for, at each level and in each industry, and which ones are changing

Pick a job. The skill map lists every skill employers ask for, the levels that need it, and whether demand is steady, rising or fading. Each call names the data behind it, and every forecast is scored when the data comes in.

Illustrative data Wages, counts, windows, probabilities and track-record figures on this page are invented to show structure. Nothing here is research output. The only real figures are in the "Current build" column of the layer model.

      Method
      
    How a call is made, and how it is checked
    
      
    A forecast here is not a guess about the future of work. It is a narrow, dated claim about what employers will ask for, built from sources whose timing is known, and scored when the next windows arrive.
    
    
    
    
      
      
    1. Know how early each source is. Job postings move first. O*NET and ESCO confirm years later. BLS projections speak to headcount, not skills. Each signal on the page carries its timing.
      2. 
      
    2. Require two kinds of evidence. Postings alone can only produce an early signal. A skill is called rising only when an independent source agrees on the direction.
      3. 
      
    3. Score every call. Each rising, early or fading skill carries a dated, checkable claim with a probability. When the window closes, the claim resolves against the posting sample and the score is published.
      4.

    What each status requires

    fadingShare down 5+ points across 2 comparable windows, same employer floor."Asked less often. Official maps may still list it."That the skill is no longer needed.

      How one posting gets a level
      
    The level comes from what the posting says the person will own. Title and years wording are weak signals. When strong signals disagree, the posting stays unassigned.

      For your review
      
    Decisions
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Settled
    
        
      
          
    • Industry grain: broad NAICS sectors. Larger sub-industries are named inside each sector.
      • 
          
    • Top performer: posting demand plus O*NET ratings, each labeled with its timing. O*NET is the slow, durable bar; postings are the current ask.
      • 
          
    • Connection threshold: lift 1.5+, from 25+ postings across 8+ employers, matching the employer floor for "rising". Lines show only in the Connections view.
      • 
          
    • Forecast horizon: 4 quarters.
      • 
          
    • Probabilities: people see the percentage, with the base rate it started from.
      • 
          
    • Ladder shape: role-specific level names on a shared L1 to L5 scale.
      • 
          
    • Emerging roles: published with a partial O*NET anchor, clearly marked experimental.
      •
