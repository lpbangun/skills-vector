      Method
      
    How a call is made, and how it is checked
    
      
    A forecast here is not a guess about the future of work. It is a narrow, dated claim about what employers will ask for, built from sources whose timing is known, and scored when the next windows arrive.
    
    
    
    
      
      
    1. Know how early each source is. Job postings move first. O*NET and ESCO confirm years later. BLS projections speak to headcount, not skills. Each signal on the page carries its timing.
      2. 
      
    2. Require two kinds of evidence. Postings alone can surface an early signal. A skill is called rising only when an independent source agrees on the direction.
      3. 
      
    3. Score every call. Each rising, early or fading skill carries a dated, checkable claim. Numeric research probabilities require a historical backtest. Without one, “No forecast yet” is explicit. Resolved calls without probabilities are tracked, never scored.
      4. 
    
    


    
    
    
    Asked-together edges require lift ≥1.5, 25+ postings and 8+ employers. Sector profiles require 30+ postings and 8+ verified-sector employers. Level pips mark a 15%+ recorded posting share. Management is a linked role, not a sixth level.
    
    
    Where each source sits in time
    
    
    Canonical source policy, not a claim that every source has been imported into this release. Skill-specific readings appear in the detail panel.

      How one posting gets a level
      
    Scope and ownership are strong signals. Seniority words and years stay weak; a named customer segment in a title counts only under a reviewed role-specific scope policy. Conflicting strong evidence remains unassigned. Recorded scope and evidence are shown below; this surface does not expose individual posting triage.
